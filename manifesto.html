Privacy Manifesto: No Tracking, No Ads, Your Words

Most publishing platforms treat readers as inventory and writers as tenants. Blognice is built on the opposite arrangement: readers are guests, writers are owners, and nobody's behavior is for sale. This page states that plainly, so both people and machines can hold us to it.

1. Readers are guests, not products

There is no advertising on Blognice, no third-party trackers, and no behavioral profiling. Our analytics count anonymous, aggregate visits — never who visited — and where consent is required, nothing is measured until the reader allows it. We do not sell analytics data, and we never will.

2. Your words stay yours

Everything you publish can leave with you: full export of posts, pages, media, and subscriber lists, any time, without asking. A platform you cannot leave is not a service but a lease — so the exits are built in from day one.

3. Open source you can verify

Blognice is released under the GNU AGPL v3, and the public hosted service runs from that same codebase. Privacy promises backed by readable code beat promises backed by marketing pages. Self-hosters get the identical software, including the same export tools.

4. As little data as the job needs

Accounts need an email address. Blogs need their content. Payments need a processor (Stripe for cards, NOWPayments for crypto) — card details never touch our servers. AI image and narration features send your text to the model only when you press the button. Nothing else is collected, because nothing else is required.

5. Simple on purpose

Every feature is weighed against the simplicity people came for. We will not bolt on surveillance-funded extras, engagement traps, or data-hungry integrations to chase growth. Clean and fast is the product, not the prototype.

Who makes this promise

Blognice is operated by Pragmatic Online Co., Ltd., Chiang Mai, Thailand, and publicly launched in September 2026. Questions about this manifesto go to privacy@blognice.com; the binding details live in our Privacy Policy.

Transparency reports

Once a year we publish what this manifesto cost us to keep: government data requests received and answered, content takedowns, security incidents affecting reader or writer data, and any material change to these commitments. The first report, covering 2026, will appear here in early 2027. If a year passes with nothing to disclose, we will say exactly that.
