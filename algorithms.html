How blognice ranks popular posts

Popular means readers genuinely spent time with a post. It does not simply mean “published most recently.”

What contributes to the score

Each night, blognice combines anonymous aggregate analytics from the previous 90 days. Unique readers are the main signal. A reader who stays for about 30 seconds and reaches roughly half the article adds an engaged-read signal, which is weighted more heavily than a quick visit.

Repeated refreshes from the same anonymous browser identifier do not count as unlimited new readers. We do not store those identifiers in the ranking database.

Why older posts can remain popular

Popularity uses a 21-day half-life. A post’s influence gradually fades unless readers continue discovering it. This keeps the section current without making it another list of the newest posts.

When a post qualifies

A post needs evidence from at least three unique consenting readers before it can appear. If a blog is new or has too little traffic, blognice does not pretend that its newest posts are popular; the section stays hidden until the evidence is sufficient.

Privacy and reliability

Only aggregate daily counts and scores are retained for ranking. Visitor identifiers remain inside the existing consent-controlled analytics system and are not copied into D1. If an analytics refresh fails, the last successful ranking remains in place rather than breaking a blog homepage.

The ranking is recalculated in a scheduled job, so loading a blog homepage is fast and does not query Analytics Engine live.
